## 1. Why This Profile Exists

Equity analysis fails in a way crypto analysis does not: the primary source is
excellent and almost nobody reads it. A company's own filings are exact, dated, amended in
public and free to retrieve, and the numbers people actually argue over come from screeners
that normalise them silently.

This profile reviews what a company filed. Every figure carries the accession number, form
type and reporting period that produced it, so two people disagreeing about a number can
settle it by opening the same document.

The gates below are the ways an equity claim goes wrong at the level of evidence rather
than of judgement. None of them is about whether a business is good.

## 2. Scope

In scope: figures a company has filed with a securities regulator, the share structure and
control disclosed in those filings, and the observable cost of exiting a position.

Out of scope, and stated because the omission is the point: whether the business is good,
whether the price is right, what the shares will be worth, and anything a company has said
outside a filing. Guidance, earnings calls and press releases are not evidence under this
profile.

**Decision Finalisation and Publication Separation** applies unchanged from RBE-001: the
actor who assembles a decision may not publish it, and neither may be the sceptical seat.

## 3. The Artefact Is a Filing

A review is pinned to a set of filings identified by accession number. That is the equity
equivalent of a block height: immutable once filed, retrievable by anyone, and precise
enough that a disagreement is resolvable.

Two consequences follow, and both are limits rather than features.

**Filings are stale by construction.** A 10-K describes a year that ended months ago. A
review of it establishes what was reported, never what is true today, and a decision under
this profile expires far faster than the document it examined.

**A period can have several filings.** The original, the amendment, the comparative in a
later report. This profile treats them as several facts rather than as one fact with
corrections applied, and the reviewer chooses.

## 4. Board Composition

Six specialist seats -- FVA RIA SDA CRA MLA RPA -- plus a Board Chair, a Methodology
Auditor and a Sceptical Reviewer. The pool, the tiers and the quorum are declared in
`PROFILE.json` and enforced by the runtime rather than by this document.

## 5. The Six Filing Gates

### SG-01 — Filing-observable verification

**Reviewer seat:** FVA

**Requirement.** Every quantitative claim must be reproducible from a named filing -- accession number, form type and reporting period -- by anyone holding the same document. A figure that cannot be traced to a filing is ALLEGED and can never establish occurrence.

**Failure mode.** A screener's number is cited as though it were the company's. Vendors apply undisclosed normalisations, and the figure cannot be regenerated by anyone else.

### SG-02 — Restatement and revision integrity

**Reviewer seat:** RIA

**Requirement.** Where a period has been filed more than once at different values, every version must be reported with the filing that produced it. No version may be selected silently, and an as-restated figure may never be compared against an as-originally-filed one.

**Failure mode.** A ratio is computed from an original 10-K and compared to a later year that was restated, so the trend is an artefact of the correction rather than of the business.

### SG-03 — Share count and dilution

**Reviewer seat:** SDA

**Requirement.** Basic and diluted counts must be distinguished, the count must be dated, and instruments issuable into shares must be enumerated. A market capitalisation must state which count it used.

**Failure mode.** Apple's FY2013 share count is filed at 899,213,000, then 899,213, then 6,294,494,000 after a retroactive split -- three answers for one period across a factor of 7,000, each equally confident.

### SG-04 — Control and related parties

**Reviewer seat:** CRA

**Requirement.** Identify every holder of disproportionate voting power, every class of share and its rights, and material related-party transactions. A claim of dispersed ownership must be verified against the filings that report control.

**Failure mode.** A company is described as widely held while a founder retains supervoting shares that decide every vote.

### SG-05 — Exit liquidity

**Reviewer seat:** MLA

**Requirement.** Liquidity must be assessed as the cost of exiting a stated position size, not as average daily volume. Borrow availability and cost are identified separately where a short is contemplated.

**Failure mode.** Average daily volume is reported as though it were depth. The question a holder faces is what leaving costs, and ADV does not answer it.

### SG-06 — Reproducibility

**Reviewer seat:** RPA

**Requirement.** Every figure must carry the filing, period and retrieval that produced it, and a sample must be re-retrieved and shown to reproduce. A figure that cannot be regenerated is a claim.

**Failure mode.** A number is quoted from memory or from an earlier draft, and nobody can tell which filing it came from.

## 6. Evidence Standards

| Tier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| T1 | A query against chain state at a recorded block height, with its result |
| T2 | Deployed contract source or bytecode, read at a recorded address |
| T3 | An artefact the system produced, with recorded provenance |
| T4 | Assertion without a retrievable referent |

**A project's own dashboard is T4, not T1.** It is a claim about chain state, not chain
state. This distinction does more work than any other rule in this document.

A T4 finding may be raised but cannot alone carry SEV-1. Agent-produced output is never
evidence; an agent seat citing its own reasoning has cited T4.

## 7. Severity Classification

| Severity | Meaning |
|---|---|
| SEV-1 | The defect makes a reported figure wrong, and a holder would rely on it |
| SEV-2 | The defect can make a figure wrong under conditions that occur |
| SEV-3 | The defect degrades clarity, traceability, or reproducibility |
| SEV-4 | Observation; no defect asserted |

The vocabulary is shared with RBM-001 and RBM-002 deliberately, so a decision means the
same thing under any of the three.

## 8. Review Tiers

| Tier | Applies to | Specialists |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A single contract, or a bounded re-review at a new block height | 2, from CVA/ADA/RPA |
| 2 | A protocol with multiple contracts, or any position being taken | 4 |
| 3 | Anything with an upgradeable proxy, a bridge, or a custodial dependency | 6 |

Upgradeability is Tier 3 by construction. A contract that can be replaced has no
guarantees that survive a review of its current implementation.

## 9. Agent Seats

Permitted for MA, the six specialists, and SR. Never for BC.

Independence is not assumed from separate invocations. It requires distinct instruction
versions per seat, recorded model and instruction version, and a sceptical seat blind to
the proposed outcome. Seats sharing a model are recorded as sharing one, because two seats
on one model are correlated reviewers.

## 10. Process Status and Decision Framework

Process status and outcome are separate axes, as in RBE-001:

- `READY` — procedurally complete; an outcome may be computed
- `PROCEDURALLY_INCOMPLETE` — quorum, evidence lock, or block height missing
- `BLOCKED` — an external dependency prevents completion
- `VOID` — withdrawn

Only `READY` produces `PASS`, `PASS_WITH_FINDINGS`, `FAIL` or `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`. The
precedence rules are RBE-001's, unchanged and unchangeable by this profile:

| Priority | Rule | Condition | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | RBM-DEC-001 | `unresolved_sev1 >= 1` | FAIL |
| 2 | RBM-DEC-002 | `unresolved_sev2 >= 2` | FAIL |
| 3 | RBM-DEC-003 | `unresolved_sev2 == 1 and accepted_sev2_remediation == 0` | FAIL |
| 4 | RBM-DEC-004 | `substantive_evidence_sufficient == false` | INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE |
| 5 | RBM-DEC-005 | `unresolved_sev2 == 1 and accepted_sev2_remediation == 1` | PASS_WITH_FINDINGS |
| 6 | RBM-DEC-006 | no unresolved SEV-1 or SEV-2, evidence sufficient | PASS |

`INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` is expected to be a common outcome here and is not a failure of the
review. Where a project's claims cannot be reproduced from chain state, that *is* the
finding.

## 11. What This Board Will Not Produce

No price target, no rating, no buy or sell signal, no score.

It produces three lists: what is established at this artefact, what is alleged and
unverified, and what could not be checked at all. Anyone converting that into a
position is making a decision the board did not make and does not endorse.

A review under this profile can never, by itself, authorise a transaction.
Authorisation requires a ratified mandate under a binding profile, which this is not.
